Fitting & Installation

Factory Toughened Rooflights are designed for straightforward installation by competent roofers, builders and glazing professionals. This guide explains the typical preparation, support, positioning and sealing requirements for our fixed frameless rooflights.

Fitting & Installation

Before You Begin

This page provides general installation guidance for Factory Toughened Rooflights. Installation should be carried out by a competent person with suitable experience of roofing, glazing and weatherproofing.

The installer remains responsible for assessing the supporting structure, roof construction, site conditions, fixing method, sealant compatibility and any project-specific requirements before installation begins.

Where structural calculations, specialist glazing design or other project-specific professional advice is required, this should be obtained before fitting the rooflight.

Important

Never stand or walk on the rooflight. Factory Toughened Rooflights are not supplied as walk-on glazing unless this has been specifically agreed and confirmed for the individual product.

Understanding Your Rooflight Size

All rooflight sizes shown on our website refer to the internal visible glass size, which is also the nominal size of the lower internal pane.

The structural opening should normally be approximately 10mm larger overall than the internal glass size, providing approximately 5mm clearance on each side.

Example

For a rooflight with an internal visible glass size of 1000 × 1000mm, the structural opening would typically be approximately 1010 × 1010mm.

Always check the actual rooflight dimensions and opening before final construction or installation.

Black Border & Rooflight Overhang

Our standard frameless rooflights are manufactured with a 100mm ceramic black border around the perimeter of the external top pane.

With a standard 100mm border, the external top pane is normally 200mm larger overall than the internal visible glass size.

Example

A rooflight with a 1000 × 1000mm internal visible glass size will normally have an external top pane measuring approximately 1200 × 1200mm.

The external top pane should extend beyond the outside edge of the supporting upstand by at least 25mm on each side.

If the upstand is wider than the standard arrangement, alternative border sizes may be required. The intended upstand dimensions and rooflight specification should therefore be checked before ordering.

Preparing The Upstand

The rooflight should be installed onto a suitably constructed, structurally sound and insulated upstand.

For a typical installation, the upstand should be approximately:

  • 150mm high above the finished roof surface.
  • Approximately 70mm wide when used with our standard 100mm border arrangement.
  • Installed with a minimum finished rooflight fall of 5°.

The 5° minimum pitch assists water run-off and helps reduce the likelihood of standing water remaining on the glass.

The roof covering or membrane should be dressed securely up the external face of the upstand in accordance with the roofing system manufacturer's requirements.

Before fitting, ensure the top of the upstand is level across its supporting surfaces, free from debris and without sharp edges, high spots or irregularities that could place uneven pressure on the glass.

Rooflight Support Batten

A timber support batten of approximately 25mm should be installed around the full internal perimeter beneath the lower pane of the stepped insulated glass unit.

The support batten helps support the internal pane, maintain the correct installation level and reduce the risk of uneven loading or unsupported glass edges.

Suitable structural glazing tape or another compatible glazing support material should be applied to the supporting surface so that the glass is cushioned and is not sitting directly against hard timber or another unsuitable surface.

The support should be continuous, secure and capable of carrying the weight of the rooflight.

Preparing For Installation

Before lifting the rooflight into position, check:

  • The structural opening dimensions.
  • The internal and external rooflight dimensions.
  • The upstand height and width.
  • The finished roof pitch.
  • The support batten and glazing support material.
  • That the roof covering has been properly dressed to the upstand.
  • That all surfaces receiving sealant are clean, dry and suitable for bonding.
  • That all sealants and tapes are compatible with the glass, insulated glass unit edge seal, roofing materials and upstand.

The rooflight should be inspected for obvious damage before it is lifted into place.

Applying The Primary Seal

Apply a continuous bead of suitable low-modulus weatherproof silicone around the full perimeter of the top surface of the upstand.

The silicone should be compatible with the rooflight, glass, upstand and surrounding roofing materials. A suitable glazing-grade product such as Dow 791, or an appropriate equivalent, may be used where compatible with the complete installation.

The bead should be continuous and sufficient to form an effective weather seal between the underside of the rooflight and the supporting upstand.

Avoid breaks, gaps or areas where the sealant cannot make proper contact.

Positioning The Rooflight

Rooflights are heavy glass products. Use enough people and suitable lifting equipment for the size and weight of the unit being installed.

Carefully lower the rooflight onto the prepared upstand without dragging the glass across the supporting surfaces.

Check that:

  • The internal pane sits correctly within the prepared opening.
  • The rooflight is centred on the upstand.
  • The black border overlaps evenly around the perimeter.
  • The external pane extends beyond the upstand by at least 25mm on each side.
  • The lower pane is properly supported by the prepared support batten.

Apply firm and even pressure around the perimeter so the primary silicone seal makes continuous contact with the underside of the rooflight.

Do not apply excessive localised pressure to the glass.

Secondary Structural Seal

Once the rooflight has been correctly positioned, a compatible structural glazing silicone should be applied to the outer underside perimeter of the rooflight where the stepped glass border meets the upstand.

A suitable structural glazing product such as Dow 895, or an appropriate equivalent, may be used where confirmed as compatible with the glass unit and surrounding materials.

The structural silicone should form a continuous perimeter bond and should be applied in accordance with the sealant manufacturer's instructions, including any requirements relating to joint size, surface preparation, primers, application temperature and curing.

Tool and finish the seal neatly without disturbing the primary weather seal or placing unnecessary pressure on the glass.

Installations Above 5 Metres

Where the rooflight is to be installed more than 5 metres above the nearest ground level, the internal or bottom pane must be specified as laminated safety glass.

The laminated pane must face the interior of the building. This requirement applies regardless of the roof pitch.

Please Check Before Ordering

If the rooflight will be installed more than 5 metres above the nearest ground level, select the laminated option when ordering. If you are unsure whether your installation requires laminated glass, please contact us before placing the order.

Sloping Roof Installations — 15° To 45°

Where a Factory Toughened Rooflight is installed at a pitch above 15° and up to 45°, the internal or bottom pane must be supplied as laminated safety glass.

The laminated pane must face the interior of the building.

At these pitches, the installer must also provide suitable support and restraint appropriate to the weight and angle of the rooflight so that the unit cannot move down the slope.

Sloping Installation

Do not rely on weatherproofing silicone alone to support the rooflight or prevent downward movement on a sloping installation. The completed installation must provide suitable permanent support for the weight of the glass.

Installations above 45°, unusually large or heavy rooflights, or installations requiring a non-standard supporting arrangement should be assessed separately before fitting.

When Laminated Glass Is Required

For Factory Toughened Rooflights, the internal or bottom pane should be laminated where either of the following applies:

  • The rooflight is installed more than 5 metres above the nearest ground level.
  • The rooflight is installed at a pitch above 15° and up to 45°.

Where both conditions apply, a laminated internal pane is still required and the additional support and restraint requirements for the sloping installation must also be followed.

Internal Finish

The internal plasterboard or other finishing system can normally be fitted after the rooflight support batten has been installed. This is generally the preferred arrangement because it makes the supporting structure easier to inspect before finishing.

Alternatively, the internal lining may be installed first where the construction allows the support batten to be fitted securely afterwards.

Maintain suitable clearance around the lower glass pane and avoid allowing plasterboard, plaster, timber or other hard materials to bear directly against the glass.

Do not permanently trap the glass against the surrounding internal finish.

Final Installation Checks

Before completing the installation, the installer should confirm:

  • The rooflight is correctly centred and fully supported.
  • There are no unsupported glass edges.
  • The required pitch has been achieved.
  • The external pane has sufficient overhang beyond the upstand.
  • The primary weather seal is continuous.
  • The secondary structural seal is continuous and properly finished.
  • All tapes and sealants used are compatible with the rooflight and surrounding construction.
  • The roofing membrane or covering is correctly dressed and sealed around the upstand.
  • There is no visible debris, hard point or localised pressure against the glass.
  • Where required by the installation height or roof pitch, the internal pane is laminated.

Where appropriate, water run-off should be checked after fitting. If water consistently remains on the glass rather than draining away, the fall and surrounding roof construction should be reviewed.

Handling & Safety

Insulated glass rooflights can be extremely heavy. The number of people and type of lifting equipment required will depend on the size, glazing specification and access conditions.

Never attempt to lift a rooflight that cannot be handled safely by the people and equipment available.

Protect glass edges from impact throughout handling and installation. Do not drill, grind, cut or otherwise alter the toughened or laminated glass after manufacture.

Suitable personal protective equipment and safe working-at-height procedures should be used throughout installation.

Installation Responsibility

Every roof construction and installation site is different. This guide describes a typical Factory Toughened Rooflights installation and is not intended to replace the professional judgement of the installer or any project-specific architectural, structural or roofing requirements.

The person carrying out the work is responsible for confirming that the supporting structure, installation method and materials used are suitable for the particular building and comply with applicable requirements.

If there is any uncertainty about the rooflight specification, opening dimensions, border size, laminated glass requirement or intended installation arrangement, please contact us before installation.
